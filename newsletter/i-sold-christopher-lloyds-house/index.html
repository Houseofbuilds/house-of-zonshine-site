I Sold Christopher Lloyd’s House (and Lost My Mind a Little)

The Sycamore House, Back to the Future, and one very surreal full-circle real estate story.

In this letter

Summer is here!

Little Me Would Be Shocked

One of the greatest gifts of this business is getting a front-row seat to people's stories. Last month, I shared a special home in the Hollywood Hills known as the Sycamore House, a property filled with creativity, character, and decades of memories. Since then, the home has officially closed escrow and begun its next chapter.

What stays with me isn't just the architecture or the history (though there was plenty of both). It's the reminder that homes become the backdrop for entire seasons of life. And this one felt a little surreal.

As a kid, I watched Back to the Future more times than I can count. If you had told me then that I'd one day be helping sell the home Christopher Lloyd lovingly transformed and made his own, and literally left a mould of his face, I never would have believed you.

Moments like this make me think about how important it is to build a life you genuinely want to live. Not because of titles or achievements, but because of the experiences, the people, and the unexpected adventures along the way.

Little Julia would think present-day Julia is pretty cool. And truthfully, I'm grateful she kept dreaming and saying yes even when it seemed impossible.

As we move into summer, I'm feeling especially grateful for the people who trust me to be part of their own next chapters.

Did you know?

  • To afford the median-priced U.S. home right now, a buyer needs a household income of about $123,000—but the median American household earns around $77,000. That's a $45,000 gap. Only two cities in the entire country—Detroit and Pittsburgh—have median incomes high enough to comfortably buy the typical home there.
  • There are several ways parents and grandparents can help younger family members buy a home: a cash down payment as a gift, a family loan, cosigning on a mortgage, buying the house outright, or inheritance. Twenty-two percent of first-time buyers used a gift or loan from family or friends to help with a down payment in 2025, down from a peak of 36% in 2010. Around 8% use inheritance. One stipulation of a recent parent buying for their kids? They had to buy within 10 minutes of their home. (WSJ)
  • One in ten Americans say they've sold their home because of paranormal activity. Some even disclose it in the listing. Anyone say roommate?
  • Petoskey, a small Northern Michigan town with fewer than 6,000 year-round residents, has joined the ranks of Nantucket and Napa as a luxury housing market where more than half of new home listings are priced at $1 million or more. (Crain’s)
