First-time buyers

First-time home buyer in Los Angeles: what to know before you buy.

Clear guidance for the money questions, the neighborhood questions, the house questions, and every decision that comes before closing.

By Julia ZonshineLos Angeles Real Estate
“The right first home is not a test you have to pass. It is a decision you get to understand.”

Buying your first home in Los Angeles starts with a clear plan for money, location, and what you are willing to compromise on—not with trying to memorize every real-estate term at once. Julia Zonshine helps buyers make sense of those decisions, spot what deserves a closer look, and move through the process with an honest picture of what comes next.

There is a lot of noise around a first purchase, especially in a city where one budget can lead to very different homes and very different daily lives. We start by getting clear about the numbers, the neighborhoods, and the non-negotiables, then build a search around your actual life.

Get the money conversation clear first.

You do not necessarily need 20% down, but you do need to understand the down payment, closing costs, monthly payment, and how competitive a particular home may be. Start with how much money you may actually need to buy in Los Angeles, including assistance programs and the questions to take to a lender.

Then figure out what you are really buying.

A home is never just the listing photos. I help buyers look at condition, disclosures, the block, daily logistics, and the things that may affect the decision once the excitement settles. The red flags I watch for on a tour and the plain-English buyer glossary are two useful places to get oriented.

You should know what to ask of your agent, too.

A buyer-agent relationship should make the process clearer, not more mysterious. Read the questions I think are worth asking before you hire an agent, including the question I wish more buyers asked: what would talk you out of this house?

First-time buyer questions

Do I need 20% down to buy my first Los Angeles home?

No. Many qualifying buyers buy with 3% to 5% down, though the right number depends on the loan, home price, monthly payment, and how competitive the offer needs to be.

What should I do before touring homes?

Start by understanding the full budget, getting clear about location and tradeoffs, and choosing an agent and lender who can explain the process before an offer feels urgent.
