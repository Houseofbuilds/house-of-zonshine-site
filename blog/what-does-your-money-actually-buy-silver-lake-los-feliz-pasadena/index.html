What Does Your Money Actually Buy, From Silver Lake to Pasadena?

The same budget buys a completely different life depending on where you spend it. Here's the honest version.

A real buyer's market and a rising median aren't a contradiction. A well-priced home still moves, an overpriced one sits, no matter the neighborhood.

The same budget buys a completely different life depending on where you spend it, and the gap between Silver Lake, Los Feliz, and Pasadena only widens as the price climbs, it never narrows.

LA crossed into a real, measurable buyer's market this past August, with close to 62% more sellers than buyers citywide, and the county median still rose 1.7% year over year that same month. That's not a contradiction, it's the actual split happening on the ground, a well priced home still moves fast no matter the neighborhood, and an overpriced one sits. The bands below are the honest version of what each price actually gets you in each place right now, checked against real current listings in each neighborhood, not pulled from one source and left there.

One honest note before you read these. What's below is an average built from everything actually trading right now, not one number handed down from one source. Every property is its own story, so expect the occasional home that breaks the pattern in either direction, a deal that undersells the area or one that overshoots it. Think of this as a rough starting point for the conversation, the kind of thing you'd want to know before you start touring, not a quote.

Silver Lake

$900K, the entry point

This is buying your way in, not your way up. The real entry point is a well kept one or two bedroom condo in the flats near Sunset Junction, somewhere around 800 to 1,200 square feet, in good shape but without the view you're picturing. An actual house at this price is close to a myth right now. If one turns up, it's small, under 1,000 square feet, and it's asking you for a full renovation before it gives you anything back.

$1.5M, the sweet spot

This is where the neighborhood starts showing you why people fight for it. Up in the hills, a two bedroom canyon home around 1,200 to 1,500 square feet usually has real character, Spanish or midcentury, a deck, and partial views, not quite postcard yet. Stay closer to the reservoir at the same price and you're trading square footage for the walk, an older bungalow that still needs cosmetic work but puts you five minutes from the loop everyone actually moved here for.

$2.2M and up, the premium tier

Past this point you're paying for the view itself. A three bedroom plus architectural home in the Moreno Highlands, 1,800 square feet and up, usually comes fully renovated with designer finishes and often a pool, looking straight at the Hollywood Sign or the reservoir. This is the tier where Silver Lake stops being a neighborhood you settle for and starts being one you chose on purpose.

Los Feliz

$1.2M to $1.4M, the real entry level

Los Feliz asks more at every level, and the jump starts right at the bottom. Getting in here now realistically means this range, and what it buys is a two bedroom condo or a small, original-condition bungalow south of Los Feliz Boulevard, the kind of honest starter home that still needs new systems and a kitchen before it's really yours.

$1.8M, two different lives on the same map

Up in Franklin Hills, a two to three bedroom home around 1,500 to 1,800 square feet gets you hillside charm and maybe a slice of the Griffith Observatory view, on a lot steep enough that the yard stays small. Down in the flats at the same price, you're more likely looking at a larger, already updated three bedroom bungalow with Hillhurst Avenue an actual walk instead of a drive.

$2.5M and up, The Oaks and Laughlin Park

Past this point you're buying into an address as much as a house. A three to four bedroom historic estate, 2,500 square feet and up, turnkey, with Craftsman or Spanish Colonial details kept intact alongside whatever's been modernized underneath. Laughlin Park itself regularly climbs well past $4M, so this is really where that tier opens its door, not where it tops out.

Pasadena

$900K, the urban option

Pasadena is the one place on this list where distance from downtown LA does more of the pricing work than any one neighborhood's reputation. At this price you're looking at a modern two bedroom condo or townhome near South Lake in Old Pasadena, or a small single family house in Northwest Pasadena that's honest about needing some cosmetic work.

$1.5M, the character home

This is where the city's signature actually shows up. In Bungalow Heaven, that's a classic three bedroom Craftsman, 1,500 to 2,000 square feet, well preserved, with the yard and historic charm that's the real reason people move here in the first place. Closer to the Arroyo Seco, the same budget gets you a smaller midcentury home or an updated cottage near Brookside instead.

$2.2M and up, the estate tier

Past this point you're in Madison Heights or up into the San Rafael Hills, a four bedroom plus historic home at 3,000 square feet or more, fully renovated or meticulously kept, with the kind of lot and privacy that starts including Rose Bowl views.

The Real Takeaway

Three neighborhoods, the same handful of price points, three genuinely different answers every time. That's really the whole point of comparing them side by side instead of picking one and hoping it fits, the right neighborhood for you isn't the one with the prettiest photos, it's the one where your actual budget buys the life you want to live in it.
