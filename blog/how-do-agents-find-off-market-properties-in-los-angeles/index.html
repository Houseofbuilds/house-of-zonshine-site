How Do Agents Find Off-Market Properties in Los Angeles?

There is an off market, and it's not a listing you'll ever find by scrolling. Here's where these homes actually come from.

By Julia Zonshine October 20, 2026
“There is an off market, and it's not a listing you'll ever find by scrolling.”

Off-market means exactly what it sounds like: the home never hits the MLS, never shows up on Zillow or Redfin, and never gets a sign in the yard. If you've ever asked how some buyers seem to land a house you never even saw come up, this is almost always the answer, and it comes down to relationships, not luck.

What "off-market" actually means

People throw around a few different terms here, and they're not all the same thing. A true off-market home is never entered into the MLS at all. A "pocket listing" is similar, held by one agent and shared quietly within their own network before it goes public, if it goes public at all. "Coming soon" is different again, a home that will be listed, just not yet, usually while photos and prep are still happening. I use all three at different points depending on what a seller actually wants, and the right one depends entirely on their situation, not a formula.

Where these actually come from

There's no secret database. What there is, is a network. Other agents who know I have a buyer looking for a very specific thing, and call me before they call anyone else. Past clients who mention, almost in passing, that a neighbor is thinking about selling. Estate situations, where a family needs to move quietly and privately before they're ready for the world to know. None of it is mysterious. It's just years of actually showing up, being easy to work with, and staying in touch with people long after a deal closes.

Why a seller would go this route

Privacy is the big one. Some sellers don't want their neighbors, their coworkers, or frankly the internet watching their home sit on a public site with a days-on-market counter ticking up. Others want to test a price quietly before committing to a public listing, which gives them real information without the pressure of a public price drop later. And some just want the whole thing to feel calmer, fewer strangers walking through on a Sunday, fewer photos floating around forever.

Why a buyer should still ask about these

Less competition is the obvious upside, you're not bidding against ten other offers on opening weekend. But I want buyers to go in with eyes open. An off-market deal can move fast, sometimes faster than a buyer expects, so it helps to already be pre-approved and clear on your numbers before one comes up. It also means doing your own homework on value, since there's no public comp trail building in real time the way there is with a listed home. That's exactly the kind of thing your agent should be walking you through before you make an offer, off-market or not.

The ones where you have to have an imagination

Not all the time, but sometimes you'll come across these listing photos and they're obviously taken on an iPhone, very, very little prep was done. Those are the ones where you have to have an imagination. I rarely sell those to clients who don't have one, not because there's anything wrong with them, some people just can't quite see past it yet, and that's completely fine. What they might not notice is the beautiful arched windows behind it, or the beams, or that terracotta floor, or whatever the case may be. But if you can see it, and it actually works out, that's often the real difference between paying today's price and owning a home that could easily be worth a couple hundred thousand dollars more once it's actually brought back to life.

How I actually work this for my clients

If a buyer tells me specifically what they're looking for, architecture, neighborhood, a feeling they can't quite name yet, I keep that in the back of my mind every time I'm out in the world, not just when I'm actively showing houses. That's also why I keep a running list of the Los Angeles homes that stop me in my tracks, because paying attention to what's genuinely special in this city, listed or not, is just part of how I stay sharp for the next person who needs exactly that.
