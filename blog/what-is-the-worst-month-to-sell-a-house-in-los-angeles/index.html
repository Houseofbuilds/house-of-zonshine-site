What's the Worst Month to Sell a House in Los Angeles?

There's a real calendar answer, and I'll give it to you. But it's not the one that actually sinks a sale.

Clients ask me this one all the time, usually while they're trying to decide whether to list now or wait. So let me give you both answers, because they're actually different things.

The Real Answer, the One Nobody Wants

The worst month to sell your house is the month you don't have the time or the energy to do it properly. That's it. That's the actual answer, more than any month on a calendar.

Selling a house is a full-time job while it's happening. It needs to be prepped, decluttered, sometimes staged, photographed well, and then shown on short notice, over and over, until it sells. If you don't have the bandwidth for that right now, whatever else is going on in your life, your house won't be prepped properly and it won't be photographed properly. And a house that goes on the market looking like that doesn't sell fast. It sits. I've watched a listing sit for six or seven months with price reduction after price reduction, and that's not a bad month, that's a house that went up before anyone was ready for it to.

So before you look at a calendar, look at your own life. Do you have the next six to eight weeks to actually do this right? That question matters more than whatever month it technically is.

The Calendar Answer, Since You Asked

Now, if you want the real data, here it is, and it's worth knowing even with everything I just said. Statewide, January is the slowest month, with homes sitting an average of 67 days. In Los Angeles specifically, December is the weak point, both for speed and for price, averaging 57 days on market. On the other end, listings in the February through June window move fastest, closer to 30 to 36 days, and July tends to bring the strongest prices, with a median sale price around $1.15 million.

So yes, if you're asking purely about the odds, winter is the slower season and spring into early summer is the strongest. That part is real, and it's worth factoring in, especially alongside where leverage actually sits in this market right now.

What That Data Doesn't Tell You

Here's where I'll push back on myself a little. People assume the holidays are dead because everyone's distracted, and on average, that's true. But there are still serious buyers out there during that slow window, the ones who specifically wait for the holidays because they know fewer people are competing for the same houses. If one of those buyers finds your house, your house sold. The slow season didn't stop that from happening, it's actually what made it happen for that one buyer who was patient enough to wait for less competition.

I'm not telling you to ignore the data. I'm telling you the data describes averages, and your house only needs one right buyer, not an average one.

So What Do You Actually Do With This

If you have real flexibility on timing and nothing else pushing you to move, the spring window is the safer bet. The data backs it up, and so does my own experience watching houses move through it. But if life has already decided your timeline for you, a job relocation, a sale that needs to close before another purchase, a family situation that can't wait for March, don't talk yourself into believing you picked a bad month. You picked the month that was actually available to you, and the thing that will determine how it goes isn't the season. It's whether the house gets the real prep and the real attention it needs before it goes live.
